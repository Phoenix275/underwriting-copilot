## §A — SHIPPED · Portfolio economics in the Executive view (UHC finance feedback)

> "You've got your projected approved premium. What's your expected payout against that
> premium? … my SG&A, all of my operating costs … where does my operating income come in?"

Approved premium alone told the executive what comes in the door, not what the book keeps.
The Executive Overview now carries a **portfolio P&L**:

- **Approved premium (annualised)** — as before, the revenue side.
- **− Expected claims payout** — illustrative actuarial model: Gompertz base mortality by
  age × each case's rule-engine relative-mortality multiple (the published weights are
  `28·ln(multiple)`, so `exp(rule_score/28)` recovers it), × a select-period factor (newly
  underwritten lives run below ultimate mortality). Capped conservatively.
- **− SG&A + acquisition** — named illustrative loading (12% of premium).
- **− Cost to underwrite the book** — per-application system cost + underwriter touch cost
  on referred cases.
- **= Expected operating income**, plus **loss / expense / combined ratios**.

All assumptions are named constants in `src/dashboard.py` and labeled illustrative in the
UI — a pilot swaps in carrier actuals to make this the real class-profile P&L.

## §B — SHIPPED · Cost-to-underwrite metric (the Telerom viability argument)

The pitch's core economic claim — automation makes small-premium policies viable — is now
visible in the product, not just said in meetings: **cost per application with the copilot
vs. an all-manual baseline**, and total underwriting cost avoided across the book. A term
policy writing a few hundred dollars a year cannot carry a ~$160 manual review; it can
carry ~$47.

## §F — Research: what an insurance operations admin desk actually owns (roadmap)

Beyond the decision feed and evidence requests, real carrier ops desks own — each a
candidate future panel:

1. **NIGO management** ("not in good order") — intake triage of incomplete/malformed
   applications, return-to-agent loops, completeness scoring. Biggest single source of
   cycle-time loss in practice.
2. **Requirements & vendor management** — ordering APS/labs/MVR/MIB, tracking vendor
   turnaround SLAs, cost per requirement, escalating stale orders. (We have ordering;
   the tracking/escalation half is the ops job.)
3. **Workforce & queue balancing** — reassigning cases when a desk backs up, capacity
   forecasting, overtime/backlog projections.
4. **SLA & TAT reporting** — cycle-time dashboards by stage, breach alerts, root-cause
   splits (waiting-on-carrier vs waiting-on-third-party).
5. **Audit & compliance packaging** — regulator-ready extracts (we have CSV/JSON),
   plus retention schedules, access logs, and periodic model-governance evidence.
6. **User & access administration** — provisioning, role changes, separation-of-duties
   checks (an underwriter can't approve their own family's case).
7. **Producer/agent liaison** — status callbacks, agent portals, chasing signatures
   and amendments.

## Explicit non-goals (scope discipline from the Telerom call)

- **No image-based fraud detection** (claims-photo comparison, damage checks) — a different
  problem domain; the validated piece is document-level inconsistency detection.
- **No external data-source dependencies** — Malaysia/Indonesia have no shared cross-insurer
  claims/health repository; the design stays **document-intake-only**.
- Retinal scans, chatbot, handwritten OCR: unchanged from the 7/24 PRD (research / P1
  as previously scoped).
